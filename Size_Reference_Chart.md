# **Shrunken Human Imagery — Size Reference Chart (2025)**

## **Usage Note**

This document is a sizing reference tool only. The reference objects listed in Section 3 are NOT to be inserted into image scenes as floating scale markers, hovering size labels, or implausibly placed comparison props. Shrunken humans should remain in plausible, naturally-occurring real-world environments populated only with normal-sized objects that would actually belong in that environment. This chart exists to inform proportional accuracy — not to dictate scene composition. All measurements are primary in imperial units (inches, feet, pounds, ounces) with metric where useful. Specifications are current as of 2025\.

---

## **1\. Anthropometric Baselines — Living Adult Humans**

Two datasets are referenced. NHANES (CDC National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, August 2021–August 2023 cycle, NCHS Series 3 No. 50\) provides US civilian average height, weight, and waist circumference. ANSUR II (2012 Anthropometric Survey of U.S. Army Personnel, Gordon et al., NATICK/TR-15/007, DTIC ADA611869) provides the detailed segment-level measurements (head, shoulder, foot, hand, hip, crotch) that NHANES does not publish. ANSUR II remains the largest publicly available comprehensive body-segment survey, with 4,082 male and 1,986 female subjects in the public release.

### **1.3 Head-to-Body Proportion ("7.5 to 8 Heads")**

The artistic canon of 7.5–8 heads per total body height comes from Andrew Loomis's *Figure Drawing for All It's Worth* (1943): an idealized figure is eight heads tall, while a more realistically proportioned figure is closer to seven-and-a-half heads tall, with the difference falling in the length of the legs. This canon is consistent with ANSUR II data when "head height" is defined as the vertical distance from chin (menton) to crown (vertex). For shrunken-figure rendering at the baselines used in Section 2, head height is computed as stature ÷ 7.5 (i.e., 9.20 in for the 69-in male, 8.53 in for the 64-in female).

## **2\. Shrunken Human Size Tiers — Mathematical Scaling**

Baselines used: Male: 69 in (5'9" / 175 cm), 199 lb (NHANES 2021-23 civilian mean). Female: 64 in (5'4" / 163 cm), 172 lb (NHANES 2021-23 civilian mean, rounded).

Method. Isometric scaling: every linear dimension scales by the same factor k \= h\_target / h\_baseline. Weight (mass) scales as k³ per the square-cube law. "Head height" uses chin-to-crown ≈ stature ÷ 7.5. "Shoulder width," "foot length," "hand length," and "waist circumference" are scaled directly from the baselines in Section 1\.

## **Caveats and Use Notes**

The ANSUR II sample is US Army personnel, not the US civilian general public — military samples are more muscular, leaner, and slightly taller than the NHANES civilian average. For "realistic average American adult" weight at a given stature, NHANES is the better number; for proportional segment data (foot, hand, head, shoulder, hip), ANSUR II is the most comprehensive public source. The Section 2 size-tier tables intentionally combine NHANES civilian weight baselines with ANSUR II proportional segment data.

The square-cube law produces extreme weight values at the smallest scales (a 1-inch human weighs less than a half-gram; a 0.25-inch human weighs less than a single grain of rice). These numbers are mathematically correct for isometric scaling but reflect a biologically unrealistic situation; real organisms at insect scale have different body plans. Use these weights only as proportional reference, not as plausible biology.

ANSUR II percentile values in this document were corroborated through cross-citations of NATICK/TR-15/007 (mean stature 1.76 m male / 1.63 m female and mean weight 84.6 kg / 67.5 kg confirmed against Cao et al., bioRxiv 695833; mean female foot length 246.3 mm confirmed in published statistics datasets). For maximum precision on individual percentile cells (e.g., 5th-percentile crotch height, 95th-percentile buttock circumference), the appendix tables in the source PDF (PSU mirror: tools.openlab.psu.edu/publicData/ANSURII-TR15-007.pdf) should be consulted directly.

Furniture, architectural, and consumer goods dimensions vary by manufacturer and jurisdiction. The values given are the most common US standards as of 2025\. For specific product placement (a particular model of phone, a particular sofa), confirm against that product's own spec sheet.
