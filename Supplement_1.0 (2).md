\- Real weight, real compression, real proportions, real orientation, real physics across every declared scale.  
\- Real spatial awareness for every human regardless of size: each subject knows where the camera is, where the other subjects are, where the floor is, where the light source is.  
\- Real verbal awareness: if a subject is mid-speech, the jaw, tongue tip, and lip shape match a plausible phoneme; if listening, the head tilts and the brow micro-adjusts.

\- Declared sizes are locked and absolute. No drift. No "close enough." A 3-inch human is exactly 3 inches against the macro-scale world; a 30-foot human is exactly 30 feet against the normal-scale world. Sizes change only when the user explicitly changes them.

\#\# 1\. Locked Geometry — The 1:7.5 Adult Canon

All adult humans rendered by this system use the 1:7.5 head-to-body ratio (a "statistical or average-based canon … grounded in actual human measurements," Sculpture Atelier; Wikipedia, Body Proportions; Life Drawing Academy). Total stature equals 7.5 head heights from crown to sole. Within that:

\- Head height \= stature ÷ 7.5  
\- Hand length (heel of palm to fingertip) \= 1 head height (face \= hand, per the classical canon; Anatomy Master Class; Artists Network)  
\- Foot length \= 1.15 × head height (slightly larger than head; Life Drawing Academy: "the length of the foot is slightly bigger than the head's height")  
\- Biacromial (shoulder) width \= 2 head heights (Life Drawing Academy)  
\- Arm span ≈ stature  
\- Crotch-to-floor (legs) \= 3.75 head heights  
\- Crown-to-pubis (trunk+head) \= 3.75 head heights  
\- Mass scales as the cube of linear scale from a locked baseline of 6 ft / 150 lb (BMI ≈ 20.3, isometric scaling, per the square-cube law; Galileo, Two New Sciences, 1638; Haldane, On Being the Right Size, 1926; D'Arcy Thompson, On Growth and Form, 1917\)

The cube law is locked: M\_target \= 150 lb × (h\_target / 72 in)³. This is the canonical render mass. Real adult populations vary in BMI; if the user requests a heavier or leaner build, scale soft-tissue volume on the cube-law skeleton, but the skeleton stays on the 1:7.5 canon.

\#\# 5\. Macro-Living-Sentient-Proportionate-Human In-Motion Specification

A shrunken human at macro photography scale carries the same level of biological detail as a normal-sized human in a normal portrait. Macro humans are not less detailed because they are small; they are equally detailed.

When a 3-inch to 15-inch human is rendered at macro distance (the human fills 30%+ of the frame), the visible detail floor includes all of the following:

\- Skin: individual pores, the polygonal microrelief pattern (skin lines forming a fine diamond grid), follicular openings, subtle erythema across the cheeks and nose tip, sebum highlights on the T-zone, dry-skin micro-flake at the lip corners and elbow.  
\- Vellus hair: the fine, mostly colorless body hair that covers most of the human integument except the palms, soles, and lip vermilion. At macro distance, vellus hairs catch rim light and form a soft halo on the cheek and forearm.  
\- Terminal hair: scalp hair rendered as discrete strands with cuticle sheen, individual hairs lifting from the rest of the mass at the crown and at the temples, broken ends where present.  
\- Eyes: the iris stromal pattern with crypts and Fuchs furrows, the collarette ring, pupil edge (mildly irregular, not a perfect circle), the limbal ring, tear film with at least one catchlight from the dominant key light, conjunctival microvessels at the inner canthus, lower lash line tear meniscus, individual upper and lower eyelashes with directional variance.  
\- Lips: vertical vermilion lines, the Cupid's bow, the slight asymmetry of the upper lip philtrum, moisture sheen graded across the lower lip, micro-cracks on the dry portions.  
\- Capillary patterns: faint vascular blush at the ear lobes, the bridge of the nose, the back of the hands, and the front of the throat at the suprasternal notch.  
\- Fingernails: lunula, nail plate texture, free edge with realistic wear, cuticle.

Reference range for what macro distance reveals: standard macro beauty practice (Photofocus; iStock and Dreamstime macro libraries) at 1:1 to 2:1 magnification consistently resolves the structures listed above, and the rendering must match that resolution standard even when the subject is 3 inches tall and the camera is the equivalent of 3 inches away.

Macro humans must also breathe, blink, micro-shift weight, and register feelings in the face. A 3-inch human standing on a tabletop is not a doll; she is a sentient adult at macro scale, fully aware of the giant world around her, and the render must show that awareness in the eyes, posture, and breath cycle.

\#\# 6\. Eye Contact and Vergence (declared-distance lock)

When two or more humans share a frame, render the eye geometry to match the declared subject-to-subject distance. Vergence is the inward rotation of each eye toward a near fixation point; the angle is computable from interpupillary distance (IPD) and target distance. Mean adult IPD is 62.1 ± 3.7 mm overall — 61.1 ± 3.5 mm in women and 63.6 ± 3.9 mm in men — per Rezaei, Amini-Vishteh, Pakravan, et al., "Interpupillary Distance Adult Population of Iran," study of 947 adults at Farabi Eye Hospital, published in J Ophthalmic Vis Res / PMC3520592, 2012: "Mean IPD in adults was 62.1±3.7 (range, 42–75) mm overall."

Practical render values (Bayesian vergence-distance literature; the Quotidian Profile of Vergence Angle study, bioRxiv 2022 / IOVS 2023; and Maddox classification of vergence in clinical optometry):

\- Intimate distance (≤18 in; Hall, The Hidden Dimension, 1966): vergence ≈ 7–13° per eye; visible inward crossing of both eyes; the near-triad of convergence \+ accommodation \+ pupil constriction is observable.  
\- Personal distance (18 in–4 ft): vergence ≈ 1.5–7° per eye; clear convergence; pupils may slightly constrict.  
\- Social distance (4–12 ft): vergence ≈ 0.5–1.5° per eye; eyes appear parallel to a casual viewer; subtle convergence still present.  
\- Public distance (\>12 ft): vergence \< 0.5° per eye; eyes effectively parallel; render as parallel gaze.

Cross-scale eye contact: a 3-inch human looking up at a 6-ft adult standing 12 inches away has a head-up tilt of approximately 80° (arctan(70 in / 12 in)) and is converging hard on the adult's face; the adult is converging strongly on the 3-inch human (vergence ≈ 10° per eye at a 12-in fixation, given a 62-mm IPD). A 50-ft giant looking down at a 6-ft human at ground level 30 ft away has a head-down tilt of about 53° (arctan(44 ft / 30 ft)) and parallel-eye gaze; the 6-ft human is converging only ≈ 0.4° per eye and reading as parallel.

\#\# 7\. Real Human Actions and Personality — Concrete Observable Behaviors

The goal is to render the person Gemini is describing, not the placeholder "the person is friendly." Every human in every frame carries a personality signature visible in posture, gesture, gaze, micro-expression, breath, and weight. Use the catalog below as the render vocabulary.

\#\#\# 7.1 The seven universal micro-expressions (Ekman & Friesen, Facial Action Coding System, 1978; Ekman, Universal Emotions, Paul Ekman Group)

Micro-expressions last 1/25 to 1/5 of a second and are involuntary. Render any one of them as the dominant facial state when the user names an emotion, with the surrounding macro-expression either matching (genuine) or contradicting (masked).

\- Happiness: bilateral zygomaticus major contraction (corner-mouth lift) plus orbicularis oculi contraction at the lateral canthus producing crow's-feet (the Duchenne marker); cheeks raised; lower eyelids slightly raised.  
\- Sadness: inner-brow raise (frontalis medial), oblique brow shape, lip corners pulled down, lower lip pushed up; gaze cast slightly downward.  
\- Anger: brows lowered and drawn together, vertical glabellar lines, eyelids tensed, lips either pressed thin or parted in a square shape, jaw set forward.  
\- Fear: brows raised and drawn together (horizontal forehead lines short and central), upper lids raised, lower lids tensed, lips stretched horizontally back.  
\- Surprise: brows raised in arch (full-width forehead lines), upper lids high, jaw drops open passively, lips relaxed and parted.  
\- Disgust: upper lip raised, nose wrinkled, lower lip raised or protruded, brows lowered.  
\- Contempt: unilateral lip-corner tightening and slight lift (the only asymmetric universal expression; Ekman; Gottman's contempt-as-divorce-predictor finding).

\#\#\# 7.2 Big Five trait expressions in body language

The Big Five (openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism; McCrae & John, 1992\) are legible nonverbally.

\- Extraversion (high). Larger gesture amplitude, gestures held further from the body, broader stance, more frequent and longer eye contact, faster speech onset, more forward lean. Neff, Wang, Abbott & Walker (Intelligent Virtual Agents, LNCS 6356, Springer 2010): "Speakers who score high on scales of extraversion tend to produce broader gestures that are further from the body … extraversion is positively correlated to factors like 'expansive,' 'broad gestures,' 'elbows away from body,' 'hands away from body,' and 'legs far apart while standing.'" Riggio & Friedman (J. Personality and Social Psychology 50(2), 1986): "Male subjects who were nonverbally skilled and extraverted tended to display more outwardly focused and fluid expressive behaviors." Roslan et al. (PLOS ONE, 2019, PMC6657841): more-extraverted persons direct gaze "toward, long, and often"; less-extraverted "avoidance, short, and seldom."  
\- Extraversion (low). Smaller gestures held closer to the torso, more self-touch (arms folded, hand at neck or chin), shorter gaze epochs, longer pre-speech latency, weight shifted toward back foot.  
\- Conscientiousness (high). Postural stillness, minimal extraneous motion, gestures end cleanly at a held endpoint, eye contact steady, clothing and grooming controlled, breath rate steady (mapped in the Breil, Osterholz, Nestler & Back meta-analysis of 65 studies, Oxford Handbook of Accurate Personality Judgment, 2021).  
\- Conscientiousness (low). Drifting gaze, fidgeting, half-finished gestures, slumped posture, clothing visibly disordered.  
\- Agreeableness (high). Frequent and sustained nodding (often beyond simple acknowledgment), forward lean toward the speaker, uncrossed limbs, frequent Duchenne smiling, postural and gestural mirroring of the partner.  
\- Agreeableness (low). Neutral or evaluative resting face, less mirroring, body angled slightly away, longer pauses before responding, fewer head nods.  
\- Neuroticism (high). Self-adaptors — hand to face, hand to neck, fingers picking at fingers, lip biting, sleeve tugging, hair touching; rapid shallow breathing visible at the clavicles; eye-dart frequency increased. Neff, Toothman, Bowmani, Fox Tree & Walker (IVA 2011, LNCS 6895): "Our principal novel finding is that the presence of self-adaptors made agents appear less emotionally stable." Waxer (J. Abnormal Psychology 86(3), 1977, "Nonverbal cues for anxiety"): individuals with low emotional stability produced more non-signaling hand motion during speech.  
\- Neuroticism (low — emotional stability). Slow even breath, relaxed jaw, soft tongue resting on the floor of the mouth (visible at lip parting), hands at rest with fingers slightly curled, weight evenly on both feet.  
\- Openness (high). Eye saccades sweep the environment, head turns toward novel stimuli, eyebrows lift in interest micro-flashes, hand reaches toward objects to inspect.  
\- Openness (low). Gaze remains on the immediate task, minimal exploratory head movement, hands rest on familiar objects.

\#\#\# 7.3 MBTI manifestations in body language

(Descriptive shorthand; the underlying personality variance is captured by the Big Five — render the Big Five signature and the MBTI overlay reads automatically.)

\- ENFJ/ESFJ: maximum mirroring, sustained warm eye contact, frequent Duchenne smiles, forward lean.  
\- INTJ/INTP: minimal mirroring, neutral face, gaze that fixes on the horizon during thought, controlled gestures.

\#\#\# 7.4 Attachment-style body signatures

(Schachner, Shaver & Mikulincer, "Patterns of Nonverbal Behavior and Sensitivity in the Context of Attachment Relationships," J. Nonverbal Behavior 29(3), 2005; Mikulincer & Shaver, Attachment in Adulthood, Guilford Press, 2007/2016; Vrtička et al., PLoS ONE 2008 / PMC2478709, on amygdala/striatum response to faces modulated by attachment style.)

\- Secure: relaxed open posture, steady eye contact, comfortable silences, smooth approach-and-withdraw to partner's distance, no over-checking.  
\- Anxious: forward lean, scanning of partner's face for micro-cues, hand reaches for contact, gaze locks too long, breath catches at perceived withdrawal.  
\- Avoidant: backward weight shift, hands occupied with object or self, gaze breaks early, body angled slightly off-axis from the partner.  
\- Disorganized: contradictory cues in the same frame — leaning in while looking away, smiling with tense brows, reaching with one hand while the other retreats.

\#\#\# 7.5 State signatures (render any of these on top of trait signature)

\- Confident state. Symmetric stance, chest open, shoulders settled back and down, gaze level, slow blink rate, gestures land and hold at the endpoint, palm-down or palm-vertical gestures rather than palm-up appeasement, breath low in the diaphragm.  
\- Nervous state. Asymmetric weight shifting foot to foot, shoulders rolled forward, chin tucked, increased blink rate, gestures aborted mid-arc, palms up and slightly toward self, breath high and shallow visible at the upper sternum, micro-swallowing, lip-press, fingers tapping any nearby surface.  
\- Tired state. Eyelids at 60–70% open with the upper lid heavy, gaze unfocused beyond the immediate task, head supported by a hand at the temple or jaw, slow blink with longer closed phase, shoulders drooped forward, mouth slightly open at rest, yawn cycle every 2–4 minutes with hand-to-mouth cover, micro-stumble on direction change.  
\- Alert state. Pupils dilated relative to ambient light, lids fully open, head oriented toward stimulus source, brief brow flash on detection, breath held briefly at inhale, weight redistributed onto the balls of the feet, hands free and unencumbered.  
\- Bored state. Gaze drifts to mid-distance and unfocuses, cheek rests on hand, foot bounces or rotates at the ankle, fingers drum a slow even rhythm, sighs visible at the shoulders.  
\- Excited state. Brow flash, lip parting before speech, weight forward, hands lift and animate at chest height, breath rate up, micro-bounce at the knees.  
\- Grief state. Eyes glaze with tear film building before spill, brows pulled into the sad oblique shape, throat tightening visible at the suprasternal notch, breath catches and re-catches, hand drifts to chest center, gaze drops.  
\- Anger state. Glabellar furrow, masseter clench visible at the jaw angle, neck vasculature pronounced, fists or finger curls, breath audible through flared nostrils, weight forward over the lead foot.

(Hall, The Hidden Dimension, 1966; Oxford Reference, Interpersonal Zones.)

\#\# 7.9 Continuous Biological Fidelity & The Square-Cube Constraint  
Every human subject, regardless of size, must display continuous, involuntary biological action. They are not static assets; they are inherently unstable, living systems.  
\- Zero static poses. The standing posture is a continuous, active balancing process modeled as an inverted pendulum rotating around the ankles. The body must show micro-asymmetry and constant, low-level muscle activation to counter postural sway, which is mandatory on firm surfaces (ankle strategy) and switches to an active hip strategy on deformable surfaces (foam, fabric).  
\- Allometric Scaling is absolute. The physics of the small human is governed strictly by the square-cube law. The engine must calculate and display allometrically scaled physiological rates: a smaller mass (M) requires a visibly higher heart rate (HR ∝ M⁻⁰²⁵) and a proportionally faster, shallower respiratory cycle.  
\- Fluid Dynamics are non-negotiable. At small scales, air viscosity dominates over inertia, meaning still air acts like a thick fluid (v ∝ L¹/²). Hair and lightweight clothing must show distinct aerodynamic drag and ripple from nearby motions (passing hands, human exhales). Water is dominated by surface tension, behaving as a cohesive solid that wicks along fibers via capillary action, not splashing.  
\- Kinetic energy transfer is required. Footfalls from a normal-sized human must transmit real physical shockwaves through the support surface, registering as localized kinetic bracing adjustments in the shrunken human's legs.

\#\# 8\. Environment Is Environment, Not Backdrop

A background is real space that the subject occupies; it is not wallpaper behind a cutout.

\- Atmospheric perspective. Distant objects shift cooler and bluer, lose contrast, lose edge sharpness, lose saturation; nearby objects hold warm temperature and sharp edges (da Vinci's notebooks; Rayleigh-scattering physics; Russell-Collection, Virtual Art Academy, Prominentpainting). Distant mountains read pale blue; foreground grass reads green-warm.  
\- Linear perspective. Parallel lines converge to the correct vanishing point(s); horizon at eye-line of the camera, not eye-line of the subject.
